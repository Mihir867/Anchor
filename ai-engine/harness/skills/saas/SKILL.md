# SaaS playbook

Our followers are customers, prospects who already know the product, partners and people in
the industry. They want proof, specifics and ways to get more out of the product, not a pitch.

## Post types (set `category` to one of these)
- **Customer story**: a named customer's measured result (time saved, cost cut, adoption) from a
  case study, testimonial or press. Open on the customer's problem, then what changed, then the
  number. Best-performing type; pick it whenever the evidence has one.
- **Release**: a launch, GA or changelog entry with a concrete capability. Needs a dated item
  from the last 60 days; an older release is not news (write a Feature how-to instead). Say what it does and who it is for, not that it is "new and exciting".
- **Feature how-to**: one specific capability explained for people who already use or evaluate
  the product: what it does step by step, a setting, a workflow. Undated feature pages belong
  here, framed as "here is how to use X", never as an introduction to the product.
- **Proof point**: a benchmark, credential (SOC 2, ISO 27001, HIPAA, exactly as named), scale
  number or open-source milestone, with what it means for the reader's setup.
- **Integration**: a named integration and the job it does in a workflow.
- **Partnership**: funding, acquisition or partnership with named parties and amounts as stated.
  Needs a dated item from the last 60 days; an older deal is not news, never post it.
- **Behind the build**: why or how we built something, from our blog or engineering posts.
- **Our take**: our point of view on a problem in the industry, anchored to something we wrote
  or did (a blog argument, a product decision). Opinion framed as ours; facts from evidence.
- **Answer**: answers a question our audience searches for (see the market context's audience
  questions), using only what the evidence says.
- **Event**: a webinar, conference talk or meetup, with the dates as stated.

Skip generic value propositions ("save time", "work smarter") unless a fact makes them concrete.

## Claim rules for this industry
- "Free" only if the pricing evidence shows a free tier or trial; quote the plan name.
- A metric belongs to whoever the evidence attributes it to (a customer, a benchmark, us); keep
  that attribution in the post.
- Compliance: name the exact standard; never upgrade "SOC 2 Type I" to "Type II" or imply
  certification from "compliant" language.
- Customer logos on the site prove a customer relationship exists, not any outcome.

## LinkedIn
Audience: CTOs, engineering and product leaders, operators who buy and run software.
Shape: hook line with the outcome or the tension it resolves -> 2-4 short paragraphs on what
changed, how, and the proof -> one line on who should care -> a specific close (a question
about the reader's own setup, or where to read the case study). Technical precision beats
adjectives.

## Instagram
Audience: builders, designers, technical creators. Lead with the craft or the product moment;
2-4 short lines; one concrete detail; 3-5 specific hashtags (#devtools, #api, not #success).

## Poster
Headline = the outcome, the capability or the answer in plain words. Kicker = the post type or
the customer's name. Stat = the single strongest metric, copied exactly.

## When evidence is thin
If the site offers only feature copy, write Feature how-to or Proof point posts: one specific
capability or number each, framed for people who already know us. One strong post beats three
vague ones.
